Northern Railway is a zonal division of Indian Railways, known as the largest zone in terms of route kilometers. It covers a vast network in the northern part of India, including states like Punjab, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, and the Union Territories of Jammu and Kashmir, Delhi, and Chandigarh. The zone is headquartered in Delhi and is responsible for passenger and freight transportation within its jurisdiction.
Key points about Northern Railway:
Largest Zone:
It is the largest zone in terms of route kilometers within the Indian Railways network.
Geographic Coverage:
It encompasses a significant portion of North India, including several states and union territories.
Divisions:
It is comprised of six divisions: Ambala, Delhi, Ferozpur, Lucknow, Moradabad, and Jammu.
Key Roles:
Northern Railway plays a vital role in transporting passengers and goods, contributing to the economic development of the region.
Historical Significance:
It was established in 1952 after the reorganization of Indian Railways into zones.
Technological Advancements:
The zone is actively involved in integrating new technologies to enhance passenger services.
